# Song backgrounds

Version 104 adds **Backgrounds / video** at Soundcheck. Choose the Riffbound concert stage, a dark stage, or a local video. Open a song first to customize its background. Iron Voltage has its own selection too.

Choose your own Guitar Hero gameplay footage, concert clip or music video. MP4 and WebM are recommended; other formats depend on the browser's codecs. Files must be non-empty. Videos up to 500 MB can be stored; larger videos remain playable for this session and must be selected again after reopening. No Guitar Hero footage is bundled or downloaded automatically.

The chooser keeps the selected filename visible and includes a muted video preview. Close it with Done to return to the chart. Invalid files show their filename and specific error; browser codec errors suggest H.264 MP4 or WebM.

Version 105 prepares the selected video before scheduling song playback: it waits for decoded frame data and a completed seek, holding video and chart during loading. Play track stops the separate chooser preview. The count-in is scheduled only after preparation; video remains paused through that count-in. Resume prepares the frozen frame before resuming audio. A video that cannot decode or finish its seek within 15 seconds keeps the song stopped and shows a video-specific retry message.

Video is always muted. The game's original song audio remains the playback clock and the chart stays above the video. Video darkness can be adjusted from 0–80%; the highway keeps its opaque surface and foreground pads. Choosing a video disables the hidden animated-band draw loop.

**Video position at first chart note** is an offset in seconds. Version 106 holds the video at its chosen starting frame through count-in and song lead-in, until the first note in the selected instrument and difficulty reaches the colored hit line. This includes the kick pedal and chords. The video clock is zero at that note; an empty chart leaves it paused. Enter 10 to skip a ten-second video intro, or -4 to delay video playback another four seconds after that note. This setting never changes chart notes or scoring timing. Opening the chooser pauses a running track. Count-ins hold the selected starting frame; preview seeks, pause/resume, restart, instrument switches, practice speed and loop wraps follow the chart transport. Seeking after the first note opens the matching elapsed video position; practice loops retain the selected full chart’s first-note origin instead of starting the video again at every pass. Device timing calibration is included so the start follows the visible hit line. Starts, pause/resume and transport changes explicitly seek to the matching video frame, including offsets shorter than 180 ms. A late-loading video catches up to the current audio position; running drift over 100 ms is corrected against the same chart clock. Small normal frame differences do not cause repeated seeks. Short videos hold their final frame rather than restarting independently.

Selections, offsets, darkness and video Blobs save per audio/song ID in the separate `riffbound-backgrounds-v1` IndexedDB database. They are local to the device and survive normal reloads and offline play. They are not part of `.riffpack` backups or the public setlist. Remove video releases its object URL and removes the stored Blob on the next committed write. Browser data clearing removes local videos. A storage failure leaves a working session copy with an explicit message; codec and playback failures do not affect chart/audio playback. Pending writes and the open dialog block automatic game reloads.

Verification: deterministic media-element tests cover transport, per-song reload, stale reads, failed writes, invalid files and late play promises. Game/DOM/audio adapter tests cover count-ins, pause, chooser keyboard isolation, preview, practice loops/speed and update safety. Browser rendering, actual video codecs and physical-device playback still need a real browser check; this environment has no supported browser QA capability.
